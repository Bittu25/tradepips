Market orders, limit orders and stop orders explained

By TradePips · Updated 10 November 2026

Market orders, limit orders and stop orders are the three tools you use to get into and out of every trade. Each fills in a different way, and the wrong choice can cost you a worse price or a missed trade. This guide explains how each order works and when to use it with a plan built on market structure.

Why order types matter

The type of order you choose decides two things:

  • Speed: does the trade happen now, or only if price reaches a level?
  • Price: do you accept whatever price is available, or do you insist on a specific one?

You cannot have both. A market order gives speed but no price control. A limit order gives price control but no promise of a fill.

Market orders: fill now at the best available price

A market order says "get me in right now." A buy fills at the current ask and a sell fills at the current bid. If you are not sure why those two prices differ, read our guide on what spread is in forex.

The risk is slippage: in a fast market, the price you see can be gone by the time the order reaches the broker. Usually it is tiny, but around news it can be several pips on EUR/USD or a few dollars on gold. Our guide on how to trade around news covers this.

Pending orders: let price come to you

Limit and stop orders are pending orders. You place them now and they wait until price reaches your level, so you do not need to watch the screen all evening. There are four basic types. Your direction and whether your level is above or below the current price decide which one you need.

Limit orders: buy lower or sell higher

A limit order fills at your price or better.

  • A buy limit sits below the current price. You want to buy cheaper, for example when price pulls back into support.
  • A sell limit sits above the current price. You want to sell higher, for example when price rallies into resistance.

The downside: if price turns a few pips before your level, you miss the trade.

Stop orders: buy higher or sell lower

A stop order turns into a market order once price touches your level.

  • A buy stop sits above the current price. You only want to buy if price breaks up through a level.
  • A sell stop sits below the current price. You only want to sell if price breaks down through a level.

Because it becomes a market order, it can slip, and it can be caught by false breakouts that poke through a level and snap back.

Your stop loss and take profit are orders too

A stop loss and a take profit are really two more orders. On a buy trade, the stop loss is a sell stop below your entry and the take profit is a sell limit above it. Whichever fills first closes the trade, and the other is cancelled.

That is why a stop loss can slip in a fast market. Our guide on how to use a stop loss explains where to place it.

Worked example: gold pullback with a buy limit

Gold is in an uptrend on the 4 hour chart. The last higher low formed at $2,340 and price is now at $2,372. Your plan is to buy a pullback into the $2,350 area, where old resistance should act as support. See support and resistance for drawing zones.

  • Entry: buy limit at $2,350
  • Stop loss: sell stop at $2,335, just below the higher low
  • Take profit: sell limit at $2,380, near the recent high

Risk = $2,350 minus $2,335 = $15. Reward = $2,380 minus $2,350 = $30. Risk to reward = 1 to 2.

If your account is $5,000 and you risk 1%, that is $50. With a contract of 100 ounces per lot, $15 of movement costs $15 per ounce, so the size is $50 ÷ ($15 × 100) = 0.03 lots, rounded down. Contract sizes vary by broker, so check yours in the lot size calculator. These prices are an illustration, not a forecast.

If price drops to $2,350, the order fills with both exits already in place. If price turns at $2,355 instead, nothing happens and you wait for the next setup.

Order expiry and other details

  • Good till cancelled: the order stays until it fills or you delete it, so review open orders daily.
  • Expiry time: many platforms remove the order at a date and time you set. Useful if a setup is only valid for one session.
  • Stop limit: some platforms offer an order that becomes a limit order, not a market order, once triggered. It avoids bad fills but may not fill at all.

Features differ between MetaTrader, TradingView, crypto exchanges and Indian broker apps, so learn how your own platform behaves.

Which order should a beginner use?

  1. Decide your entry, stop and target from the chart first.
  2. If price is far from your entry, use a pending order.
  3. Use a limit order when you expect a reaction at a level, and a stop order when you expect a break through it, ideally after a break of structure.
  4. Use a market order only when price is already at your level.
  5. Always attach the stop loss when the order is placed, not later.

Order types do not make a trade profitable. Most retail traders lose money, so practise each one on a demo account first. If you would like to learn entries and exits with a mentor in live classes, see the course details.
