Multiple timeframe analysis: from the daily chart to your entry chart

By TradePips · Updated 26 October 2026

Multiple timeframe analysis means looking at the same market on more than one chart before you trade. The daily chart tells you the direction. A middle chart shows you where to wait. A small chart shows you when to act. This guide explains how to pick your three timeframes, what to look for on each one, and walks through a full example with real numbers.

Why one chart is not enough

A 15 minute chart can show a clean downtrend while the daily chart is in a strong uptrend. If you only look at the small chart, you sell into what is really a short pullback on the bigger picture. That is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

Bigger timeframes carry more weight because each candle holds more trading. A daily swing low is built from a full day of buying and selling. A 5 minute swing low is built from five minutes. When the two disagree, the bigger chart usually wins over time, though not always.

The three charts and their jobs

Keep it simple. Use three timeframes and give each one a single job.

  • Higher timeframe (direction): is the market structure making higher highs and higher lows, lower highs and lower lows, or neither?
  • Middle timeframe (zone): where is the pullback likely to stall? Mark the nearest support or resistance zone in line with the bigger trend.
  • Lower timeframe (entry): once price reaches the zone, does the small chart turn back in the direction of the big trend?

How to choose your timeframes

A common rule of thumb is to keep a gap of roughly four to six times between each chart. Pick a set that fits how much time you can spend at the screen.

A multiple timeframe analysis example on GBP/USD

The prices below are made up to show the method.

  1. Daily chart. GBP/USD has made higher highs and higher lows for three weeks. The last higher low is 1.2600 and the last high is 1.2800. Direction: look for buys only.
  2. 1 hour chart. Price pulls back from 1.2800. The last 1 hour breakout level, an old resistance that price broke through, sits at 1.2700 to 1.2710. You mark that as the buy zone and wait.
  3. 15 minute chart. Price drops into 1.2705. On the 15 minute chart it has been making lower highs on the way down. Then a candle closes above the last 15 minute lower high at 1.2725. That is a change of character back in the direction of the daily trend.
  4. Plan. Entry 1.2728. Stop below the 15 minute swing low at 1.2698, which is 30 pips. Target just under the daily high at 1.2790, which is 62 pips.

Reward ÷ risk = 62 ÷ 30 ≈ 2.1

That gives a risk to reward of about 1 to 2. Our guides on placing a stop loss and the risk to reward ratio explain both numbers. To keep the loss at a fixed share of your account, put the 30 pip stop into the lot size calculator.

If the 15 minute chart had kept falling through 1.2700 instead, there would be no trade. The zone failed, and you simply wait for the next setup. Skipping a trade costs you nothing.

Common mistakes

  • Too many charts. Flicking between six timeframes until one agrees with you is not analysis. Three is enough.
  • Going against the higher timeframe. A sell signal on the 15 minute chart inside a daily uptrend is a pullback, not a new trend, until the daily structure changes.
  • Entering before price reaches the zone. The middle chart tells you where to wait. Buying halfway down the pullback gives you a wider stop and a worse price.
  • Changing the direction chart during the day. Decide the bias before the session and stick with it until a daily candle closes and changes the structure.

Does it work on gold and crypto?

Yes. Gold (XAU/USD) and Bitcoin follow the same highs and lows, so the same three step process applies. Both often move further per candle than major forex pairs, so stops in points or dollars are usually wider. Crypto trades all week, so its daily candle close time depends on your broker or exchange. Check what time your platform closes the daily candle in IST.
