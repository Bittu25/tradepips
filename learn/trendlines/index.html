Trendlines: how to draw them and when they fail

By TradePips · Updated 27 October 2026

Trendlines are straight lines drawn across the swing lows of an uptrend or the swing highs of a downtrend. They help you see the slope of a trend and where a pullback might stall. This guide shows you how to draw trendlines that are useful, how to combine them with market structure, and the warning signs that a trendline is about to fail.

What a trendline is, and what it is not

A trendline is a visual guide. It joins two or more swings so you can see the angle of the move and roughly where the next swing might form.

  • In an uptrend, draw the line under the higher lows.
  • In a downtrend, draw the line over the lower highs.

A trendline is not a wall and not a signal on its own. Unlike a horizontal support or resistance zone, a trendline moves with time, so the price it points to changes with every candle. Two traders often draw the same trendline slightly differently. That is normal, and it is why you should treat a trendline as an area, not an exact price.

How to draw trendlines step by step

  1. Find the trend first. Read the market structure on the daily or 4 hour chart. If you cannot see clear higher lows or lower highs, there is no trend to draw.
  2. Pick two clear swings. In an uptrend, choose two obvious higher lows with a real move up between them.
  3. Connect them and extend the line to the right. This is where price may come back to.
  4. Wait for a third touch. Two points will always make a line. A third reaction near the line is what tells you other traders may be watching it too.
  5. Be consistent. Use wicks or candle bodies, and stick with your choice.
  6. Do not force it. If you have to cut through several candle bodies to make the line fit, it is not a good trendline.

A worked example on gold

Here is a made up sequence on the 4 hour chart of gold (XAU/USD). The prices only show the method.

  1. Gold makes a higher low at 3,980, then another at 4,010 six days later. You join them and extend the line. It rises about 5 dollars per day.
  2. Four days later the line sits near 4,030. Price pulls back to 4,033, prints a higher low and turns up. That is the third touch.
  3. You do not buy just because price touched the line. You wait for the 15 minute chart to show buyers stepping in, for example a higher low and a break of the last small swing high.
  4. Your stop goes below the higher low, not just below the line, for example at 4,018. That is 15 dollars below the 4,033 low and gives price room to wobble through the line.

If your entry is 4,040 and your stop is 4,018, your risk is 22 dollars per ounce. On a standard lot of 100 ounces that is 2,200 dollars, which is far too much for most beginner accounts. Lot sizes and contract specs vary by broker, so use the lot size calculator to size the trade so that loss is a small, fixed share of your account.

When trendlines fail

Every trendline breaks eventually. Watch for these signs that a break is more likely:

Warning signWhat it suggests
The line is very steepThe move is fast and hard to sustain. Steep lines break more often.
Bounces from the line get smallerBuyers (or sellers) are losing strength at each touch.
The last high fails to breakNo new higher high is often the first sign the trend is tiring.
Price closes beyond the last swingA change of character matters more than the line itself.
The line is on a small timeframe onlyA 5 minute trendline can break inside a daily trend that is still intact.

A broken trendline is not an automatic reversal

Price often breaks a trendline and simply goes sideways. Sometimes it breaks and then the old trend resumes at a gentler angle. A cautious approach is to wait for structure to confirm. In an uptrend, look for a close below the last higher low, then a lower high. Only then does the trend look like it has changed.

Common trendline mistakes

  • Drawing the line you want to see. Draw from the obvious swings, not to justify a trade you already like.
  • Redrawing after every break. If you keep moving the line to keep it valid, the line is telling you nothing.
  • Too many lines. One or two trendlines per chart is plenty. Ten lines means every price is "on a line".
  • Stops right on the line. Wicks often poke through. Place your stop beyond the swing, as covered in how to use a stop loss.
  • Ignoring volatility. Gold and Bitcoin can move through a line by a wide margin in one candle, especially around big news. Spreads also widen at some hours and vary by broker.

Trendlines on forex, gold and crypto

The method is the same on all three, because a trendline only uses highs and lows. Forex pairs often respect lines more neatly in the busiest hours; the session chart shows when those are in India Standard Time. Crypto trades all week, so its lines include weekend candles that forex charts do not have.

Practise before you rely on them

Draw trendlines on old charts, scroll forward and note how price reacted. Then backtest your rules by hand and try them on a demo account. Most retail traders lose money, and a well drawn line does not change that on its own; your risk control does.
