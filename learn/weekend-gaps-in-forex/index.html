Weekend gaps in forex and crypto explained

By TradePips · Updated 23 November 2026

Weekend gaps in forex happen when the market closes on Friday at one price and opens on Monday at a different price, with no trading in between. On the chart it looks like a jump. For a beginner holding a trade over the weekend, that jump can skip straight past a stop loss. This guide explains why gaps happen, how they differ in crypto, and simple rules to keep them from hurting your account.

What a weekend gap is

Forex trades around the clock from Monday to Friday, then stops for the weekend. The world does not stop with it. Elections, central bank comments, wars and big company or government news can all happen on a Saturday or Sunday. When the market reopens, the first price reflects everything that changed, not where Friday ended.

If Friday closed EUR/USD at 1.0850 and the first Monday price is 1.0790, there were simply no trades between those two prices. That empty space is the gap, 60 pips in this case.

When forex closes and reopens in IST

The forex week is usually counted from 5 pm New York time on Sunday to 5 pm New York time on Friday. India does not change its clocks, but the US does, so the IST times shift:

PeriodMarket closesMarket reopens
US winter time (about November to March)Saturday about 3:30 AM ISTMonday about 3:30 AM IST
US summer time (about March to November)Saturday about 2:30 AM ISTMonday about 2:30 AM IST

These are approximate. Each broker sets its own hours, and gold often opens a little later than currency pairs. Check the trading hours in your platform's symbol specification. The session chart shows how the weekday sessions line up in IST, and our guide on the best time to trade forex in India covers them in detail.

How a gap skips your stop loss

A normal stop loss is an instruction to close your trade at the next available price once your level is touched. During a gap, the next available price may be far beyond your level. This is called slippage. Our guide on how to use a stop loss explains why stops are not a guaranteed price.

Worked example: EUR/USD

You buy 0.10 lots of EUR/USD at 1.0850 on Friday. Each pip is worth $1. Your stop is at 1.0820, 30 pips away, so your planned risk is $30.

Planned loss = 30 pips × $1 = $30

On Monday the market opens at 1.0790. Your stop triggers and fills there.

Actual loss = 60 pips × $1 = $60

You lost double what you planned, on a trade you sized carefully. On an oversized trade with no stop, the same gap can trigger margin calls.

Worked example: gold

Gold reacts strongly to weekend headlines. Suppose you buy 0.10 lots of XAU/USD, which is 10 ounces at most brokers, so each $1 move is worth $10. Your stop is $10 below your entry, a planned risk of $100. If gold opens $20 lower on Monday, the stop fills $20 away and you lose $200. Contract sizes vary by broker, so check yours before you trade. Our position sizing for gold guide shows the maths.

Weekend gaps in crypto

Crypto exchanges trade every hour of every day, so spot Bitcoin and Ethereum on a crypto exchange do not usually gap on Monday. That does not make weekends safe:

  • Thinner trading. Weekend volume is often lower, so a large order can move price sharply and spreads can widen.
  • No one watching. Price can move a long way while you are away, and your stop can still slip in a fast move.
  • Broker products can gap. Crypto CFDs at some forex brokers close or trade on reduced hours at weekends, and regulated futures such as CME Bitcoin futures stop for the weekend. These can open on Monday at a different price.

How you hold crypto, and what is allowed, depends on your country and your broker. Our guide on how crypto differs from forex covers more of the differences.

Do gaps always fill?

You may hear that "gaps always fill", meaning price returns to Friday's close. Sometimes it does, sometimes within hours, sometimes weeks later, and sometimes not at all. A gap caused by real news can be the start of a new move. Do not treat a gap fill as a rule, and do not trade one without a stop loss and a clear reason from market structure.

How to manage weekend gap risk

  • Close day trades before Friday night. If your plan is intraday, be flat before the weekend.
  • Hold smaller if you hold at all. Swing traders often cut size on trades held over the weekend, so even a gap of twice the stop is an affordable loss.
  • Plan for the worst case. Ask what you would lose if price opened two or three times your stop distance away. If that number scares you, the position is too big.
  • Check the weekend calendar. Elections and major meetings are often known in advance.
  • Know what your broker offers. Some brokers offer guaranteed stops, usually at a cost. Availability varies by broker and country.
  • Wait for Monday to settle. Spreads are often wide in the first minutes after the open. Let them return to normal before entering.

Use the lot size calculator to size each trade from your stop and a fixed risk, often 1% of the account or less.
