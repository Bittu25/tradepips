What is market structure in trading?

By TradePips · Updated 10 October 2026

Market structure is the pattern of highs and lows that price leaves behind as it moves. If you can read that pattern, you can tell whether a market is rising, falling or going sideways without a single indicator on your chart.

The building blocks: swing highs and swing lows

Price never moves in a straight line. It pushes, pauses, pulls back and pushes again. Each turning point is called a swing.

  • Swing high: a peak, where price stopped rising and turned down.
  • Swing low: a trough, where price stopped falling and turned up.

Everything else in market structure is just a way of comparing one swing with the one before it.

The three states a market can be in

  • Uptrend: each swing high is higher than the last, and each swing low is higher than the last. Buyers are in control.
  • Downtrend: each swing high is lower than the last, and each swing low is lower than the last. Sellers are in control.
  • Range: highs and lows form at roughly the same levels. Neither side is winning.

Break of structure and change of trend

A break of structure happens when price moves beyond the most recent swing in the direction of the trend. In an uptrend, that means closing above the last swing high. It tells you the trend is continuing.

A change of trend starts when price breaks the swing that was holding the trend up. In an uptrend, that is the last higher low. One break is a warning. A lower high followed by a lower low is confirmation that the market has turned.

How to mark structure on your own chart

  1. Open a clean chart with no indicators and zoom out until you can see at least 100 candles.
  2. Mark the obvious peaks and troughs. If you have to squint to see a swing, it is too small to matter.
  3. Label each one as a higher high, higher low, lower high or lower low compared with the swing before it.
  4. Decide the state: uptrend, downtrend or range.
  5. Mark the one level that would prove you wrong: the last higher low in an uptrend, or the last lower high in a downtrend.

Use more than one timeframe

A market can be in an uptrend on the daily chart and a downtrend on the 15-minute chart at the same moment. Neither is wrong. The higher timeframe shows the main direction, and the lower one shows where you might enter. Most beginners get hurt by trading a small move against the bigger trend.

Common mistakes

  • Marking every tiny wiggle. Stick to the swings you can see at a glance.
  • Treating a wick as a break. Wait for a candle to close beyond the level.
  • Ignoring the higher timeframe. Check it first, every time.
  • Trading structure without a stop. Structure tells you where you are wrong. Put your stop there.

Why this matters more than indicators

Most indicators are calculated from past prices, so they tell you what already happened, a little late. Structure is the price itself. It works the same way on forex, gold and crypto, and on every timeframe, which is why the whole TradePips course is built on it.
